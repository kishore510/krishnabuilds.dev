# The Sun at the Centre

Status: built. How the Sun moved to the centre of the sky, 270 BCE to 1687, in 8 chapters: Aristarchus's Sun-centred idea on Samos, Ptolemy's Earth-centred model at Alexandria, Aryabhata's spinning Earth at Kusumapura, the Tusi couple at Maragha, Copernicus at Frombork, Kepler's ellipses at Prague, Galileo's telescope at Padua and Newton's gravity at Cambridge. 5 people cards (Aryabhata, Copernicus, Kepler, Galileo, Newton).

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `world` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter. Dotted yellow lines are a book or an idea travelling, not a person. The four European places are close together on this map, so no route joins Frombork, Prague and Padua.
- Source: every card is **History**.
- Native names: one, the Aryabhatiya (chapter 3), in Devanagari.
- Sources and what is disputed: the astronomers’ own works in translation (Archimedes’ Sand Reckoner for Aristarchus, Ptolemy’s Almagest, the Aryabhatiya, De revolutionibus, Astronomia nova, Sidereus nuncius, the Principia) and standard histories of astronomy. How Copernicus learned of the Tusi couple and Ibn al-Shatir’s models is debated, so no line is drawn from Maragha to Frombork. That Aryabhata worked at Kusumapura rests on his own verse that his knowledge was honoured there; some scholars place him elsewhere. Whether his models hint at the Sun at the centre is argued; the card says he kept the Earth at the centre.
