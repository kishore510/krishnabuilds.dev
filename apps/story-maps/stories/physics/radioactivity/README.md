# Radioactivity

Status: built. The discovery of radioactivity, followed from laboratory to laboratory, 1895 to 1939, in 14 chapters: Röntgen's X-rays at Würzburg, Becquerel's fogged plate, the Curies' name and two elements, Rutherford's alpha and beta rays, atoms that change, the price of radium, the nucleus at Manchester, the neutron, the Joliot-Curies' artificial radioactivity, Fermi's slow neutrons, Hahn and Strassmann's barium, Meitner and Frisch's walk in the snow, the news reaching America, and what radioactivity gave us. 4 people cards (Röntgen, Rutherford, Meitner, Fermi).

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `europe` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter. Dotted yellow lines are news or a letter travelling, not a person. Work done in Montreal and Chicago is off the map; Rutherford's voyage leaves by a hidden waypoint in the Atlantic.
- Source: every card is **History**, except the last, on today's uses, which is **Science**.
- Native names: none.
- Sources and what is disputed: the discoverers' own papers and Nobel lectures, and standard histories of nuclear physics. Whether Lise Meitner should have shared Otto Hahn's Nobel Prize is still argued; the card says the decision is criticised. The death toll at Hiroshima and Nagasaki is given as “well over 100,000; estimates vary”. The one-in-8,000 figure is Geiger and Marsden's own, for their 1909 measurement. The life of Marie Curie is its own story map, in Lives.
