# The Gene

Status: built. How genetics was found, from a monk's peas to gene editing, followed from laboratory to laboratory, 1856 to 2025, in 12 chapters: Mendel's peas at Brno, Miescher's nuclein at Tübingen, Avery's DNA at the Rockefeller Institute in New York, Photograph 51 at King's College London, the double helix at the Cavendish in Cambridge, the genetic code at the National Institutes of Health in Bethesda, Dolly the sheep at Roslin, the human genome (Washington), ancient DNA at Leipzig, CRISPR at Berkeley, and a labelled aside on what Jurassic Park gets right and wrong, with the Dominican amber mines and Colossal Biosciences in Dallas. 7 people cards (Mendel, Miescher, Avery, Franklin, Khorana, Pääbo, Doudna and Charpentier).

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `world` basemap and the shared art library, with two new glyphs, `sheep` (Roslin) and `mammoth` (Dallas). It has no legs: no place has a documented journey worth drawing. Routes are set to show only the current chapter's.
- Source: History by default; chapters 11 and 12 (the aside on de-extinction) are tagged **Science**.
- Native names: chapter 1 (Brünn), in German.
- Sources and what is disputed: Mendel's 1866 paper; Miescher's 1871 report; Avery, MacLeod and McCarty (1944); Watson and Crick (Nature, 1953) with Franklin and Gosling's and Wilkins' companion papers; Nirenberg and Matthaei (1961); Wilmut and colleagues (Nature, 1997); the International Human Genome Sequencing Consortium (2001, 2004) and the 2022 gap-free assembly; Green and colleagues (Science, 2010); Jinek, Chylinski, Fonfara, Hauer, Doudna and Charpentier (Science, 2012); Allentoft and colleagues (Proceedings of the Royal Society B, 2012) on DNA decay. Disputed: how much credit Franklin and Gosling are owed for the helix and how Photograph 51 reached Watson; the count of Mendel's plants and whether his ratios were too good; whether Colossal's 2025 "dire wolves" are de-extinction (the company says yes; many scientists and the IUCN's canid specialists say they are edited gray wolves). De-extinction news moves fast: chapter 12 is accurate as of 2025.
