# The Journey of Zero

Status: built. How zero travelled from a Babylonian placeholder and an Indian dot to Brahmagupta's rules, Baghdad and Fibonacci's Pisa, c. 300 BCE to 1202 CE, in 7 chapters: the Babylonian gap in the wedges, the Maya zero in the forest, the Bakhshali dot on birch bark, Brahmagupta making zero a number, the Indian numerals reaching Baghdad, Leonardo learning the figures at Bugia, and the Liber Abaci bringing zephirum to Europe. 3 people cards (Brahmagupta, al-Khwarizmi, Leonardo of Pisa).

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `world` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter. Dotted yellow lines are a book or an idea travelling, not a person. Babylon's pin is moved about 2 degrees south-east so it does not sit under Baghdad's; the story's `note` says so.
- Source: every card is **History**, except the Maya chapter, which is **Archaeology**.
- Native names: Devanagari on chapters 3 and 4 (`sa`) and for Brahmagupta.
- Sources and what is disputed: standard histories of mathematics (Ifrah, Plofker’s Mathematics in India, Sigler’s translation of the Liber Abaci) and the 2017 Bodleian radiocarbon report. The Bakhshali manuscript’s date is disputed: radiocarbon dates its leaves to between the 3rd and 10th centuries, and many scholars date the text itself to the 8th to 12th; the card gives both. That the Indian astronomer in Baghdad around 773 brought a work drawn from Brahmagupta’s comes from later Arabic sources. The Maya zero was independent and did not reach the Old World.
