# Indian Philosophy

Status: built. In Philosophy, the subject's first story. How Indian thinkers argued about the self, the world and how we know anything, from c. 1500 BCE to 1657, in 16 chapters: the Hymn of Creation, "You are that", Janaka's contest, Mahavira, six rival teachers, the Buddha, the Greeks at Taxila, the six views, Nagarjuna, Nalanda, Shankara, the four monasteries, Ramanuja, Madhva, the new logic of Mithila and Dara Shikoh's Great Secret. 4 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `south-asia` basemap, shared with the Indian Chronicles. Routes show only for the current chapter. Shankara's four monasteries are drawn with a dashed route, as tradition.
- No new art: it uses the `sacred-fire`, `hermitage`, `palace`, `stupa`, `trading-post`, `shrine`, `palm-leaf`, `gopuram`, `mountain`, `village` and `dome` glyphs and the `hoplite`, `monk` and `scroll` traveller icons.
- Source: **History**, except chapters 3 and 12, which are **Tradition**.
- Native names are in Sanskrit (`sa`), written in Devanagari; Xuanzang is in Chinese (`zh`) and chapter 16 and Dara Shikoh in Persian (`fa`).
- Sources and what is disputed: the Rigveda and the older Upanishads (Brihadaranyaka and Chandogya) are quoted from their own texts, but their dates are estimates. Gargi, Yajnavalkya and Janaka are known only from the Upanishad, so chapter 3 is tagged Tradition. The six teachers come from the Buddhist Samannaphala Sutta; Gosala from Jain and Buddhist texts, both hostile to him. The Buddha's and Mahavira's dates are disputed by about a century. The Taxila meeting is told by Greek writers (Arrian, Plutarch, Strabo, after Onesicritus); Pyrrho's link to India comes from Diogenes Laertius. The six schools' root texts cannot be dated closely. Nagarjuna's home and dates are uncertain. Nalanda's founding follows its archaeology and Xuanzang. Shankara's dates and his travels and four monasteries come from hagiographies written centuries later. Ramanuja's 120-year life is traditional. Madhva's dates have two reckonings. Gangesha's dates are uncertain (usually the 14th century). Where Dara Shikoh's translation was made, Delhi or Varanasi, is given variously.
