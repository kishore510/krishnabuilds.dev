# The Pala Empire

Status: built. Bengal and Bihar from about 750 to 1200 CE: Gopala chosen to end the "law of the fishes", Dharmapala and the race for Kannauj, the monasteries of Vikramashila, Somapura and Nalanda, Mahipala's revival and the Chola raid, Atisha's journey to Tibet, the Kaivarta revolt, the Senas and the fall of the monasteries. 10 chapters, 4 people cards and a **Powers** layer that changes lands chapter by chapter.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `south-asia` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes of earlier chapters stay on the map (`journey`).
- Source: History throughout.
- Native names are in Bengali (`bn`); the Kannauj, Vikramashila and Nalanda chapters are in Hindi (`hi`) and Tholing is in Tibetan (`bo`).
- Sources: Pala copper plates and inscriptions (the Khalimpur plate of Dharmapala, Devapala's Nalanda and Monghyr plates), Chola inscriptions of Rajendra I, the Sena Deopara inscription, Sandhyakara Nandi's Ramacharita, Tibetan histories (Taranatha) and Atisha's biographies, Minhaj-i-Siraj's Tabaqat-i Nasiri, Dharmasvamin's account, and excavation reports for Paharpur, Antichak and Nalanda; standard histories by R. C. Majumdar (History of Bengal, vol. 1) and others. Disputed: Pala regnal dates (often a decade or more apart between historians), how Gopala was chosen, how far Devapala's claimed conquests reached, the exact identity of the monastery Bakhtiyar Khalji sacked, and who destroyed Vikramashila and when.
