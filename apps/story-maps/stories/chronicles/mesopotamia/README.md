# Mesopotamia

Status: built. The land between the Tigris and the Euphrates, from the carved pillars of Göbekli Tepe (about 9500 BCE) and the first farmers at Jarmo, through Eridu, Uruk and the invention of writing, the rival cities of Sumer and the Royal Cemetery of Ur, Sargon's empire of Akkad and its trade with Dilmun, Magan and Meluhha, the Third Dynasty of Ur, Mari and Hammurabi's Babylon, the Hittites, the Kassites and the Amarna letters, Assyria from Nimrud to the fall of Nineveh, and Nebuchadnezzar's Babylon to Cyrus, Alexander and the last cuneiform tablets, to the rediscovery from the 1840s and the damage since 2003. 27 chapters, a **Powers** layer that changes chapter by chapter, and 19 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `near-east` basemap (added for this story, and meant for Egypt and Greece and Persia too) and the shared art library. Routes show only for the current chapter.
- Source: chapters rest on **Archaeology** (excavations), **History** (texts written in cuneiform and, for the rediscovery, the modern record) or **Tradition** (Gilgamesh).
- Dates before about 1500 BCE follow the usual (middle) chronology and are approximate. Native names are in Latin letters (Sumerian `sux`, Akkadian `akk`, Elamite `elx`, Old Persian `peo`, Egyptian `egy`), because cuneiform fonts can't be relied on.
- Five pins are moved a little from their true spots so they can be told apart: Eridu (about 40 km from Ur), Nineveh and Nimrud (30 km apart), and Lachish and Jerusalem. The story's note says so.
- Powers areas are loose regions cut to Natural Earth 1:50m coastlines (public domain), so they follow today's coasts; each piece shows only in its own chapters.
- Coasts and rivers are drawn as they are today. The Gulf's ancient shoreline and the Euphrates' older course past Ur and Uruk are not drawn.
