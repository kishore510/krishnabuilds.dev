# The Holy Roman Empire

Status: built. The empire of the German kings and emperors, from Charlemagne’s crowning in 800 as its prologue and Otto I in 962 to its end in 1806: Canossa, Barbarossa and the Lombard League, Frederick II, Charles IV and the Golden Bull, the Council of Constance, the Habsburgs, Luther at Worms, the Thirty Years’ War, Westphalia, the Diet at Regensburg, Austerlitz and the dissolution. 14 chapters, a **Powers** layer that gains and loses lands chapter by chapter, and 6 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `mediterranean` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter. Many route points are hidden waypoints that keep marches on land; every leg is a march, so no pin is drawn for them and none crosses the sea. Charlemagne and Otto I cross the Alps on land to Rome, Henry IV crosses by the Mont Cenis side, Jan Hus rides from Prague to Constance and Luther travels from Wittenberg to Worms by Erfurt and Frankfurt.
- Source: every chapter is tagged **History**. Sources: Liudprand of Cremona and Widukind for Otto I, Lampert of Hersfeld for Canossa, Otto of Freising for Barbarossa, Frederick II’s own Constitutions and Matthew Paris, the Golden Bull of 1356 itself, the acts of the Council of Constance, Luther’s own writings and the record of Worms, the treaties of 1648 and the instrument of dissolution of 1806. Disputed: Luther’s “Here I stand”; the death toll of the Thirty Years’ War; how far Westphalia changed the state system; whether the 800 coronation belongs in the empire’s history (the story treats it as the prologue, as the empire’s own writers did).
- Native names are Latin (`la`, set for the whole story); the people cards for Otto I, Frederick II, Charles IV, Luther and Francis II give German names and set `lang` to `de`.
- Powers: one realm, the Holy Roman Empire, painted loosely. Areas are rough outlines cut to the basemap’s coastlines (Natural Earth, public domain), so they follow today’s coasts; each piece shows only in its own chapters. Chapter 1 shows Charlemagne’s Frankish lands, from the Spanish March to Saxony and Bavaria and Lombard Italy; chapter 2 the East Frankish kingdom with northern and central Italy to Rome; chapters 3 to 5 add Bohemia and Burgundy (from 1033), and chapter 5 the Kingdom of Sicily. Chapters 6 to 10 show Germany, Austria, Bohemia, Silesia, the Low Countries and northern Italy, with Sicily and Burgundy dropped. In chapter 11 the Swiss, the Dutch and Alsace drop out, chapter 12 keeps that, chapter 13 drops the Rhine states (the Confederation of the Rhine, 1806) and chapter 14 shows only Austria and Bohemia. The label stays on southern Germany.
