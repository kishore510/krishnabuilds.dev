# The Sasanian Empire

Status: built. The empire of the Sasanians from Ardashir’s round city and Shapur I’s capture of a Roman emperor to the death of Yazdegerd III and Ferdowsi’s Book of Kings: Naqsh-e Rostam, Roman captives at Bishapur, Mani, Shapur II and Julian, the walls of Derbent, Khosrow I’s great arch, Khosrow’s Better Antioch, the books brought from India, Adur Gushnasp, the last war with Rome, al-Qadisiyyah, Nahavand and Merv. 17 chapters, a **Powers** layer that changes chapter by chapter, and 8 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `near-east` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter. The story sets its own whole-map view (`overview`), from the Bosporus and Egypt to the map’s east edge. India lies beyond the east edge, so Burzoy’s route runs in from a hidden waypoint at the edge; the Euphrates crossing at Zeugma, Armenia and Medina are hidden waypoints too.
- Source: chapters rest on **History**: Sasanian rock inscriptions (Shapur I’s at the Kaaba of Zoroaster, Kartir’s) and coins; Ammianus Marcellinus for Shapur II and Julian; Procopius and Agathias for Khosrow I, Antioch and the Greek philosophers; Theophanes and the Armenian Sebeos for the last war; al-Tabari and other Arabic and Persian histories for the founding, Khosrow I, the conquest and Yazdegerd III. The walls and Adur Gushnasp rest on **Archaeology** (the Gorgan Wall and Derbent excavations; the German digs and clay seals at Takht-e Soleyman). Disputed: who built the Taq Kasra; the size of the medical school at Gundeshapur; the date of al-Qadisiyyah and its site; how Yazdegerd III and Boran died; whether Bozorgmehr existed.
- Native names are in Latin letters (Middle Persian `pal` by default; Persian `fa-Latn`, Syriac `syc-Latn`, Arabic `ar-Latn` and Latin `la` where a step or realm says so).
- Powers areas are loose regions cut to the basemap’s Natural Earth 1:50m coastlines (public domain), so they follow today’s coasts; each piece shows only in its own chapters. The Sasanians grow from Persis to Iran, Mesopotamia, Armenia and the Caucasus, reach Syria, the Levant and Egypt in the last war, and shrink to the north-east round Merv as the caliphate takes the rest; where two powers meet, the land is cut from the one the chapter is not about.
