# The Roman Empire

Status: built. Rome under the emperors, from Augustus in 27 BCE to the last emperor in the West in 476: Teutoburg Forest, Britannia and Boudica, Vesuvius and Pompeii, Trajan’s conquest of Dacia, the empire at its widest in 117, Hadrian’s Wall, Alexandria and the grain fleet, Marcus Aurelius on the Danube, the third-century crisis, Diocletian, Constantine, the sack of Rome in 410 and Odoacer in 476. 14 chapters, a **Powers** layer that grows and shrinks chapter by chapter, and 6 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `mediterranean` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter. Many route points are hidden waypoints that keep marches on land and fleets on water; Claudius’s crossing of the Channel is a short fleet leg between two marches, and the Tiber mouth is hidden, so no pin is drawn for them.
- Source: every chapter is tagged **History**. Sources: Tacitus (Annals, Histories, Agricola), Suetonius, Cassius Dio, Pliny the Younger’s letters, Marcus Aurelius’s Meditations, Ammianus Marcellinus, Augustine’s City of God, Ptolemy, inscriptions and coins, and archaeology. Disputed: the Teutoburg site and the Roman dead; Boudica’s death toll; the traditional date of the Vesuvius eruption; the Antonine Plague’s toll; the number of soldier emperors; whether the Edict of Milan was an edict; and what exactly ‘fall’ means in 476.
- Native names are Latin (`la`, set for the whole story).
- Powers: one realm, the Roman Empire, painted loosely. Areas are rough outlines cut to the basemap’s coastlines (Natural Earth, public domain), so they follow today’s coasts; each piece shows only in its own chapters. The Augustan empire (chapters 1 and 2) covers Italy, Spain, Gaul to the Rhine, the Danube provinces, Greece, Anatolia, Syria and Judaea, Egypt and the North African coast to the Atlas. Southern Britain is added in chapter 3 and extended to Hadrian’s Wall in chapter 7; Dacia is held in chapters 5 to 9; Trajan’s short-lived Mesopotamia appears in chapter 6 only. In chapter 10 a gap opens in northern Gaul and the Syrian desert east of Antioch drops out. Chapter 11 and 12 show the empire without Dacia; chapter 13 shows the West reduced (Italy, Gaul south of the Loire, Spain and Africa); chapter 14 shows only Italy, with a dot for Rome. The East is left to the Byzantine Empire story.
- Route choices: Claudius marches from Rome over the Alps and across Gaul to Boulogne, sails the short Channel crossing and marches on to Londinium. Trajan marches from Antioch down the Euphrates to Ctesiphon. The grain fleet sails south of Crete and Sicily to the mouth of the Tiber. Aurelian, Diocletian and Constantine march through the Danube lands round the head of the Adriatic, so no march crosses the sea; the Bosporus is closed by the basemap’s simplified coast.
