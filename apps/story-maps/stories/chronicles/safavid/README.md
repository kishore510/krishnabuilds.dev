# The Safavid Empire

Status: built. The Sufi shrine at Ardabil and Ismail’s crowning at Tabriz through Chaldiran, the Ardabil carpet, Humayun’s refuge at Qazvin, Shah Abbas’s Isfahan, the shah’s walk to Mashhad, the Armenians of Julfa, the taking of Hormuz and the winning and losing of Baghdad, to the travellers of Isfahan, the Afghan siege of 1722, Nader Shah’s crowning on the Mughan plain and the Shia Iran the Safavids left. 14 chapters, a **Powers** layer that changes chapter by chapter, and 8 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `near-east` basemap and the shared art library, with no new glyphs. Routes show only for the current chapter. The story sets its own whole-map view (`overview`), from eastern Anatolia and Iraq to the map’s east edge. Kandahar lies beyond the map’s eastern edge, so the routes of chapters 5 and 12 run in from a hidden waypoint at the edge; the Ottoman march of chapter 3 comes in from a hidden waypoint in Anatolia.
- Catalogue: the Safavid Empire entry opens the story.
- Source: chapters rest on **History**: Safavid court chronicles, above all Iskandar Beg Munshi’s History of Shah Abbas the Great (Tarikh-e Alam-ara-ye Abbasi); Ottoman accounts of Chaldiran and Baghdad; Jauhar and Gulbadan Begum for Humayun’s exile; English East India Company records for Hormuz; Jean Chardin, Pietro della Valle and other travellers; the Ardabil carpet’s own inscription and the surviving buildings of Isfahan. Disputed: whether Safi al-Din was Sunni or Shia; how forced and how fast Iran’s conversion to Shia Islam was; the numbers of Armenians deported from Julfa and of those who died; Isfahan’s population; the death toll of the 1722 siege; whether the Ardabil carpets were made for the shrine.
- Native names are in Persian script (`nativeLang: "fa"`); the Ottoman realm’s name is in Ottoman Turkish (`ota`); Chardin’s people card has no native name.
- Powers areas are loose regions cut to the basemap’s Natural Earth 1:50m coastlines (public domain), so they follow today’s coasts; each piece shows only in its own chapters. The Safavids grow from Azerbaijan to all of Iran and the southern Caucasus, gain the Gulf coast, Hormuz and (briefly) Baghdad, and shrink to the north-west in 1722; where two powers meet, the land is cut from the one the chapter is not about.
