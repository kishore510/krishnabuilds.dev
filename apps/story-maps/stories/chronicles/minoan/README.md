# Minoan Crete

Status: built. Europe's first palace civilisation, from the first farmers at Knossos about 7000 BCE to the hill refuges of about 1000 BCE: the gold tombs of Mochlos, the first palaces at Knossos, Phaistos and Malia, the peak sanctuary on Juktas, the undeciphered scripts and the Phaistos Disc, the great palaces, Gournia, Zakros, the men of Keftiu in Egypt, Minoan ways overseas, Akrotiri and the eruption of Thera, the Mycenaean takeover of Knossos, the Linear B tablets, Karfi, the Dictaean Cave, Minos and the Minotaur in Greek tradition, and Evans's and others' digging at Knossos. 20 chapters, a **Powers** layer (Egypt and Mycenaean Greece) and 7 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `near-east` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes build up over the story (`journey`). It sets its own whole-map view (`overview`) from Crete to Thebes.
- Source: chapters are **Archaeology** by default; the Egyptian evidence (chapter 11) and the digs (chapter 20) are **History**; chapters 18 and 19 are **Tradition**.
- Native names are Latin transliterations (Greek `grc` by default; Egyptian `egy` in chapter 11, Mycenaean Greek `gmy` in chapter 16).
- Powers areas are loose regions, not borders: Egypt is the Nile valley and delta to Aswan; Mycenaean Greece is the eastern and southern Peloponnese with Attica and Boeotia, plus central Crete in chapters 15 and 16. There is no realm for Minoan Crete itself.
- Sources and disputes: the story follows the archaeology of Crete as summarised in standard works: Arthur Evans's The Palace of Minos, the Cambridge Companion to the Aegean Bronze Age (ed. Shelmerdine, 2008) and the Oxford Handbook of the Bronze Age Aegean (ed. Cline, 2010), with site details from the excavators of Gournia, Zakros, Akrotiri and Karfi. Minoan dates are relative, from pottery styles, tied to Egypt and radiocarbon, so all are approximate. Disputed: the date of the Thera eruption (about 1600 BCE by radiocarbon, about 1500 BCE by archaeological synchronisms); who or what caused the destructions of about 1450 BCE; when the Knossos Linear B archive burned (about 1375 BCE or as late as about 1200 BCE); whether Minoan-style towns overseas were colonies; and the identification of Keftiu with Crete, which most but not all scholars accept. Card 18 and 19 tell Greek tradition, not history. Native names are later Greek or Linear B forms, since the Minoan language is unknown.
