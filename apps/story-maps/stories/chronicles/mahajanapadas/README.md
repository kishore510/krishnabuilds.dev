# The Mahajanapadas and the Buddha

Status: built. The sixteen great kingdoms of northern India and the teachers who walked among them, from the 6th to the 4th century BCE: the new cities of the Ganga plain, Gandhara under Persia, the first coins, Avanti and its fierce king, Udayana and Vasavadatta, the republics and Mahavira, the Buddha's birth, leaving home, awakening, first teaching and years at Rajagriha and Shravasti, Bavari's pupils from Assaka, the fall of the Shakyas, Mahavira's release, the Buddha's last journey, the eight shares of ashes, the first council, Magadha's rise over its rivals and Bodh Gaya today. 23 chapters, a **Powers** layer (Magadha growing, Kosala, Vatsa, Avanti, the Vajjis and Gandhara) and 5 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `south-asia` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter.
- Source: Buddhist (Pali canon: Anguttara Nikaya, Digha Nikaya's Mahaparinibbana Sutta, Sutta Nipata, Vinaya) and Jain texts (Kalpa Sutra, Bhagavati Sutra) are **Tradition**: they were passed on by memory and written down centuries later. Darius I's inscriptions and Herodotus are **History**; Kaushambi's ramparts, Northern Black Polished Ware and the punch-marked coins of Bhir Mound are **Archaeology**.
- Native names are in Sanskrit (`sa`), in Devanagari.
- Disputed: the dates of the Buddha (traditional 624-544 or 563-483 BCE; many historians now about 480-400 BCE) and of Mahavira (Jain tradition 599-527 BCE), the lists of the sixteen kingdoms, the sites of Kapilavastu (Tilaurakot or Piprahwa), Mahavira's birthplace (Vasokund or Lachhuar) and Pava (Pavapuri or near Kushinagar), the date of Kaushambi's ramparts and when coinage began. The Udayana story comes from much later plays and tales. Powers areas are loose regions, not borders.
