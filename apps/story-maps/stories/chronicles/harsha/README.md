# Harsha’s Empire

Status: built. Harsha’s Empire, from about 600 to about 650 CE: the Pushyabhutis of Thanesar, Rajyashri’s marriage to the Maukhari Grahavarman, Prabhakaravardhana’s death, the murder at Kannauj, Rajyavardhana’s fall, Harsha’s accession, the Kamarupa alliance, the rescue in the Vindhya forest, the throne of Kannauj, the war with Valabhi, the stand on the Narmada against Pulakeshin II, Shashanka of Gauda, the Banskhera copper plate, Xuanzang at Nalanda, Kajangala, the assemblies of Kannauj and Prayag, Harsha’s plays, the Tang envoys, the usurper and the fight over Kannauj afterwards. 21 chapters, a **Powers** layer (the Pushyabhutis, the Maukharis, Gauda, Kamarupa, the Chalukyas of Vatapi and the Maitrakas of Valabhi) and 10 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `south-asia` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter.
- Source: Bana’s Harshacharita, Xuanzang’s Record of the Western Regions and Huili’s life of Xuanzang, and the Chinese court histories are **History**; the Banskhera copper plate is **Archaeology**. Native names are in Sanskrit (`sa`), in Devanagari, with Chinese (`zh`) for Xuanzang, Wang Xuance, the Kannauj assembly and the usurper.
- Disputed: the identity of the Malava king (usually Devagupta), the date of the Narmada battle (about 618 or about 630), the date of Shashanka’s death, Xuanzang’s army and assembly figures, the authorship of the three plays, and the scale of Wang Xuance’s campaign. Bana and Xuanzang were both partisans of Harsha, and Xuanzang of Buddhism. Powers areas are loose regions, not borders.
