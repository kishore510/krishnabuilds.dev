# The Portuguese Empire

Status: built. How Portugal built the first global sea empire, from the capture of Ceuta in 1415, down Africa’s coast and round the Cape to India, through Goa, Malacca, Macau, Brazil and the Atlantic slave trade, to the Carnation Revolution and the handover of Macau in 1999. 24 chapters on a world map, a **Powers** layer (the Portuguese empire, the Kingdom of Kongo, the Vijayanagara Empire, the Spanish Empire, the Dutch Republic and Brazil) and 10 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data (places, chapters, routes, timeline, powers, people). It uses the `world` basemap and the shared art library. Routes show only for the current chapter (`routes: "chapter"`).
- Source: history. Facts follow standard histories of the Portuguese Empire, of the Estado da Índia and of the Atlantic slave trade. Disputed or uncertain points are flagged in the cards: whether Cabral’s landfall in Brazil was chance or design; the date of Vasco da Gama’s birth; the identity of the pilot who guided da Gama from Malindi (not named here, as sources disagree); the naming of the Cape of Good Hope (a tradition); and the fate of King Sebastian. The figure of about 5.8 million people carried by Portuguese and Brazilian ships is a modern estimate from the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database; the number who reached Minas Gerais is a rough estimate. The ‘school at Sagres’ is presented as probably a legend.
- Powers areas are loose regions cut to Natural Earth 1:110m coastlines (public domain), so they follow today’s coasts; small holdings are dots. Each piece is shown only in the chapters it belongs to. Portugal’s empire in Asia was mostly forts and ports, so it is shown mostly as dots.
- Lisbon, Ceuta and Alcácer Quibir; Luanda and Mbanza Kongo; Goa and Diu; Salvador and Porto Seguro; Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo and Ouro Preto are close enough that their dots touch; the map note says so. No chapter shows two of them in full.
