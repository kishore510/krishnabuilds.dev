# The Three Crowned Kings

Status: built. The early Tamil kingdoms of the Cheras, Cholas and Pandyas (the three crowned kings, Moovendar), about 1000 BCE to 600 CE, and the rediscovery of their poems: the urn burials of Adichanallur and the brick town at Keezhadi; Megasthenes' Pandaia, Ashoka's second rock edict at Erragudi, the Mangulam cave inscriptions, Elara of Anuradhapura and Kharavela's league of Tamil states; the Sangam assemblies (tradition), the poems of love and war, the Cheras of Vanci and the Pugalur inscriptions, Karikala at Venni and his port of Puhar, Nedunjeliyan at Talaiyalanganam, Avvaiyar and Athiyaman; the Roman trade at Muziris, Pliny, the Muziris papyrus, Arikamedu and the pearls of Korkai; the Tale of the Anklet, Puhar lost to the sea, the Kalabhras, the printing of the poems and Tamil as a classical language. 24 chapters, a **Powers** layer and 12 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `south-asia` basemap and the shared art library (the palm-leaf glyph and merchant ship traveller were added for this story). Routes show only for the current chapter.
- Source: excavated sites are **Archaeology**; inscriptions, the Sangam poems, Greek and Roman writers are **History**; the Sangam assemblies, Megasthenes' Pandaia legend, the Mahavamsa's Elara and the epics are **Tradition**.
- Native names are in Tamil (`ta`) by default, with Greek, Odia, Hindi and Sinhala where they belong.
- Sangam dates are uncertain and given as ranges or "early centuries CE". Keezhadi's dates are given as the state's estimate and called debated.
- Nearby sites share a pin: Adichanallur uses Korkai, Keezhadi and Mangulam use Madurai, Talaiyalanganam is a hidden waypoint. Karur, Venni and Arikamedu are nudged a little so their pins do not overlap, and the note says so. The three kingdoms' areas are loose, fixed outlines; the Kalabhras' area is the whole Tamil country because their extent is unknown.
- The successor story is The Chola Empire.
