# The Partition of India

Status: built. The Partition of India, from the Lahore Resolution and the Simla talks to the Cabinet Mission, Direct Action Day, Noakhali and Rawalpindi, the 3 June Plan, the referendums, the Radcliffe Line, independence in Karachi and Delhi, the trains, columns and camps of Punjab, Delhi and Calcutta in 1947, Junagadh, Kashmir and Hyderabad, Gandhi’s death, Chandigarh, Bangladesh in 1971 and the Attari–Wagah border. 23 chapters, a Powers layer and 8 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `south-asia` basemap and the shared art library. Routes show only for the current chapter.
- Sources: the Lahore Resolution, the Cabinet Mission and 3 June statements, the Indian Independence Act and the Radcliffe awards; the leaders’ speeches (Jinnah on 11 August, Nehru on 14 August 1947); British transfer-of-power records; survivors’ testimony gathered by oral-history projects and the Partition Museum; and modern historians. Disputed: every death toll (Direct Action Day, Noakhali, Bihar, Punjab, Hyderabad, 1971) and the total of a few hundred thousand to two million dead; the 10 to 20 million displaced; the number of abducted women; whether Mountbatten swayed the Gurdaspur award; the size of the Lyallpur column. Native names are in Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Bengali and Gujarati as each place or person suits. Powers areas are loose regions, not borders, and Kashmir is left unpainted.
