# The Nayakas of Madurai

Status: built. The Tamil country from about 1371 to 1801: Kumara Kampana's conquest of Madurai, Nagama and Viswanatha, the palayam system, news of Talikota, Roberto de Nobili, the Rock Fort and Toppur, Tirumala Nayaka's palace, the war of noses, Thanjavur and Ekoji, Rani Mangammal, Queen Meenakshi and Chanda Sahib, the poligars and the towers of the Meenakshi temple. 13 chapters, 6 people cards and a **Powers** layer that changes lands chapter by chapter.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `south-asia` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes of earlier chapters stay on the map (`journey`).
- Source: History throughout.
- Native names are in Tamil (`ta`), except chapter 1 in Sanskrit (`sa`) and chapter 4 in Kannada (`kn`).
- Sources: Gangadevi's Madhura Vijayam, Tamil and Telugu chronicles such as the Madura Tirupani Malai and the Mackenzie manuscripts, the Jesuit letters of the Madurai mission (edited by J. Bertrand, La Mission du Maduré), Nayaka inscriptions and the buildings themselves; standard histories by R. Sathyanatha Aiyar, History of the Nayaks of Madura (1924), and K. A. Nilakanta Sastri, A History of South India. Disputed: the story of Nagama and Viswanatha, and whether the king who sent them was Krishnadevaraya or Achyuta Deva Raya; the number 72 for the palayams; the dates of Toppur (1616 or 1617) and of the war of noses; when Mangammal's regency ended and how she died; the brick-in-the-Quran story; and whether Queen Meenakshi died in 1736 or 1739.
