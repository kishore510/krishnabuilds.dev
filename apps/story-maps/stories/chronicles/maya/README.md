# The Maya

Status: built. From Aguada Fénix (about 1000 to 800 BCE), El Mirador, the San Bartolo murals and Kaminaljuyu, through the Classic kingdoms: Tikal's Stela 29 (292), the arrival of Sihyaj K'ahk' (378), Copán's founder, Tikal's defeat (562), Calakmul and the Snake kingdom (Kaanul), Pakal of Palenque, Dos Pilas, Lady Six Sky of Naranjo, Tikal's victory over Calakmul (695), the Yaxchilan lintels, the beheading of 18 Rabbit at Quiriguá (738), Copán's Hieroglyphic Stairway and the Bonampak murals. Then the southern collapse and Toniná's last Long Count date (909); Uxmal, Chichen Itza, Mayapan, Tulum, Q'umarkaj and the Popol Vuh, and Columbus's trading canoe (1502); the Spanish conquest from Champotón (1517), Alvarado in the highlands (1524) and Mérida (1542) to Landa's burning of the books at Maní (1562) and the fall of Nojpetén (1697). It ends with Stephens and Catherwood, the Caste War, the decipherment, the 2018 lidar survey and the Maya today. 35 chapters, a **Powers** layer that changes chapter by chapter, and 18 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `mesoamerica` basemap and the shared art library. Routes show only for the current chapter.
- Source: the first chapters are **Archaeology**; the Classic chapters are **History**, from inscriptions as read today; later chapters are **History** from Maya books written after the conquest (the Popol Vuh, the Books of Chilam Balam), Spanish letters and chronicles, and modern records. Disputed figures (Caracol's population, the 2018 lidar population estimate, the dead of the Caste War and of Guatemala's civil war) are given as estimates and said to be disputed; Tecún Umán is given as K'iche' tradition.
- Classic dates are given to the year or month, not the day: converting Long Count dates depends on a correlation constant, and the usual ones differ by two days.
- Native names: Classic Maya names (`emy`, Epigraphic Mayan) by default, with Yucatec Maya (`yua`), K'iche' (`quc`), Itza' (`itz`) and Spanish (`es`) where they fit.
- Real distances are large enough that no pins needed moving apart; the nearest pair, Yaxchilan and Bonampak, are about 25 km apart.
- Powers areas are loose outlines, not borders: small kingdoms are drawn as rounded areas around their capitals, and the larger areas are drawn by hand over the `mesoamerica` coastlines.
