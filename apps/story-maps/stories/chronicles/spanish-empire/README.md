# The Spanish Empire

Status: built. How Spain’s crowns built the first empire on which the sun never set, from the fall of Granada and Columbus’s voyage in 1492, through the conquest of the Aztec and Inca states, silver and the Manila galleons, to independence in the Americas and the last colony in Africa in 1976. 24 chapters on a world map, a **Powers** layer (the Spanish and Portuguese empires, the Aztec and Inca empires, the Inca state at Vilcabamba and the Dutch Republic) and 13 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data (places, chapters, routes, timeline, powers, people). It uses the `world` basemap and the shared art library. Routes show only for the current chapter (`routes: "chapter"`).
- Source: history. Facts follow standard histories of the Spanish Empire and of the conquests of Mexico and Peru. Disputed points are flagged in the cards: which island Columbus first reached; how many people lived in the Caribbean and the Americas before 1492 and how many died (estimates of the population collapse range very widely, with epidemics the main cause); accounts of the death of Moctezuma; the numbers killed at Cajamarca, in the rebellion of Túpac Amaru II and at Annual. Western Sahara’s status is still disputed.
- Powers areas are loose regions cut to Natural Earth 1:110m coastlines (public domain), so they follow today’s coasts; small holdings are dots. Each piece is shown only in the chapters it belongs to.
- Palos, Seville, Cádiz and Granada; Tordesillas and Madrid; Lima, Cusco and Ayacucho; and Tenochtitlan and Dolores are close enough that their dots touch; the map note says so. No chapter shows two of them in full.
- The Manila galleons crossed the Pacific from Acapulco; the map shows only their last leg, from Guam.
