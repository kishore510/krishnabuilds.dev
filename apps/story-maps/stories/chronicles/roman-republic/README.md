# The Roman Republic

Status: built. Rome from the traditional founding in 753 BCE and the expulsion of the kings, through the consuls and the Twelve Tables, the conquest of Italy, Pyrrhus, the three wars with Carthage (Hannibal’s crossing of the Alps, Cannae, Zama and the fall of Carthage), the Gracchi, Marius and Sulla, Caesar in Gaul, the Rubicon and the Ides of March, to Actium and Augustus in 27 BCE. 14 chapters, a **Powers** layer that grows chapter by chapter, and 6 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `mediterranean` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter. Many route points are hidden waypoints that keep marches on land and fleets on water; Epirus and Lilybaeum are hidden too, so no pin is drawn for them.
- Source: chapters rest on **History** (chapter 1 is tagged **Tradition**). Sources: Livy (Roman history, composed under Augustus), Polybius (the Punic Wars and Rome’s rise, writing in Greek about 150 BCE), Plutarch’s Lives, Appian, Suetonius, and Caesar’s own Commentaries on the Gallic War and the civil war. Disputed: the traditional dates for the kings and early Republic (set down centuries later); the Caudine Forks, Zama and Rubicon sites; Hannibal’s Alpine pass; every ancient count of army sizes and dead at Cannae and elsewhere; the salting of Carthage (a later legend).
- Native names are Latin (`la`, set for the whole story). Hannibal’s name is given as in the brief, in a Latin-letter form of the Punic name.
- Powers areas are loose regions cut to the basemap’s coastlines (Natural Earth, public domain), so they follow today’s coasts; each piece shows only in its own chapters. The Republic grows from Latium (chapters 1 and 2) to central Italy (3, with Samnium added in 4), the peninsula south of the Arno–Rubicon line with Sicily, Sardinia and Corsica (from 5), the east coast of Spain (from 8), the province of Africa and Macedonia and Greece (from 9), western Anatolia and the coast of Gaul (10), the Po plain and all Gaul to the Rhine (from 11) and Egypt (14 only). The outlines were drawn as rough polygons and clipped to the land.
- Route choices: Zama is inland, so Scipio’s fleet sails to a hidden landing near Utica and his army marches on to Zama. In chapter 12 Caesar marches down the Adriatic coast, crosses by sea from Brundisium to Dyrrhachium and marches on to Pharsalus. In chapter 14 Octavian’s army marches to Brundisium and his fleet crosses to Actium, because the Strait of Messina is too narrow for the basemap’s coast. Fleets leaving Rome start from a hidden waypoint at the mouth of the Tiber.
