# The Mughal Empire

Status: built. The Mughal Empire, from Babur's boyhood in 1494 to the exile of the last emperor: Babur in Ferghana, Samarkand won and lost, Kabul, the first battle of Panipat, Khanwa and Babur's gardens at Agra; Humayun's defeats by Sher Khan, Sher Shah's roads and rupee, Akbar's birth at Umarkot, Humayun in Persia and his fall on the library stairs; Hemu and the second Panipat, the Amber alliance, Chittor, the ride to Gujarat, Haldighati, Fatehpur Sikri, the House of Worship, Todar Mal and the Ain-i Akbari, Chand Bibi and Prince Salim's revolt; Guru Arjan, Sir Thomas Roe and Nur Jahan, the gardens of Kashmir, the Taj Mahal and Shahjahanabad; the war of succession, Shivaji (Pratapgad, the escape from Agra, Raigad), Aurangzeb's stricter rule, the Khalsa, Golconda and Aurangzeb's grave at Khuldabad; the successor states, Nadir Shah, the third Panipat, Shah Alam II, 1857 and Bahadur Shah Zafar's exile, and the Mughal legacy. 39 chapters, a **Powers** layer (the Mughals changing chapter by chapter, the Lodis, the Uzbeks, Safavid Persia, the Sur Empire, Mewar, Vijayanagara, the Deccan Sultanates, Portuguese India, the English East India Company, the Marathas, the successor states, Nadir Shah and the Durranis) and 24 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `south-asia` basemap and the shared art library, which gains two glyphs for this story: a Mughal dome (`dome`) and a four-part garden (`garden`). Routes show only for the current chapter. Ferghana and Samarkand lie north of the basemap's frame; their pins sit on its painted highlands.
- Source: almost every chapter is **History** (the Baburnama, Gulbadan's Humayun-nama, the Akbarnama and Ain-i Akbari, the Jahangirnama, European travellers, Sikh, Maratha and Rajput sources). Traditions inside a chapter (Chetak, the five beloved of the Khalsa, the baskets of sweets, Aurangzeb's caps) are labelled as such in the text.
- Native names are in Persian (`fa`), the Mughal court language, with Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Punjabi, Urdu and Kannada where the place or person belongs to that language.
- Army sizes and death tolls (Panipat, Chittor, Nadir Shah's massacre, the third Panipat) are given as the sources state them and called disputed. Powers areas are loose regions, not borders.
