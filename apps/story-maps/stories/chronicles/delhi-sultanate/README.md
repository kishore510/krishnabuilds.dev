# The Delhi Sultanate

Status: built. The Delhi Sultanate, from the Ghurid conquest to Panipat in 1526: Ghazni and the battles of Tarain, Muinuddin Chishti at Ajmer, the Qutb Minar, Bakhtiyar Khalji, Aibak, Chinggis Khan on the Indus, Iltutmish, Razia and Balban; Alauddin Khalji's raids, markets and southern campaigns; the Tughlaqs, Ibn Battuta, the breakaway kingdoms and Firuz Shah; Timur's sack of Delhi; the regional sultanates, the Sayyids and the Lodis; Kabir and Guru Nanak; and the sultanate's legacy.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `south-asia` basemap and the shared art library, with no new drawings. Routes show only for the current chapter. Ghor lies west of the basemap's frame and is only a route waypoint.
- Source: every chapter is **History** (Minhaj-i Siraj's Tabaqat-i Nasiri, Amir Khusrau, Barani, Afif, Ibn Battuta's Rihla, Timur's memoirs and modern historians). Traditions inside a chapter (Prithviraj's legendary end, Muinuddin's arrival, ‘Delhi is still far’, Padmini, Kabir and Sikandar Lodi) are labelled as such in the text.
- Native names are in Persian (`fa`), the sultans' court language, with Hindi, Telugu, Tamil, Arabic, Bengali, Gujarati, Marathi, Kannada and Punjabi where the place or person belongs to that language.
- Army sizes, treasure and death tolls (Chittor, Malik Kafur's plunder, Timur's prisoners, Panipat) are given as the sources state them and called disputed. Powers areas are loose regions, not borders.
