# The Parthian Empire

Status: built. The empire of the Arsacids from Arsaces and the Parni at Nisa in the 240s BCE to Ardashir’s rise in 224 to 226 CE: Hecatompylos and Antiochus III, Mithridates I’s conquest of Media and Mesopotamia, Ctesiphon, the envoys of Han China, the king of kings at Behistun, Carrhae, Antony and Augustus, Tiridates crowned by Nero, Trajan at the Gulf, Hatra’s walls and Nisa dug up. 13 chapters, a **Powers** layer that changes chapter by chapter, and 6 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `near-east` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter. The story sets its own whole-map view (`overview`), from Anatolia to the map’s east edge. China and the lands beyond the east edge are hidden waypoints, so the Han envoys’ route runs in from off the map; Zeugma, Persis and the road to Rome are hidden waypoints too.
- Source: chapters rest on **History**: Polybius for Antiochus III at Hecatompylos; Strabo and Justin for the Parni and Arsaces; Babylonian astronomical diaries for Mithridates I in Babylonia; Sima Qian’s Records of the Grand Historian (Shiji) for the Han embassy; Plutarch for Sulla, Carrhae and Antony; Tacitus for Rhandeia; Cassius Dio for Trajan, Severus and Hatra; Pliny for Tiridates’s journey; Josephus for Musa. Hatra and the last chapter rest on **Archaeology** (the Hatra excavations and the Soviet and Italian digs at Nisa). Disputed: the origins and dates of Arsaces; the year Parthian dating begins from; army sizes and losses at Carrhae and in Antony’s campaign; the site of the battle of Hormozdgan; kings’ reign dates, which are often approximate.
- Native names are in Latin letters (Parthian `xpr` by default; Ancient Greek `grc`, Old Persian `peo`, Aramaic `arc`, Armenian `hy-Latn` where a step says so).
- Powers areas are loose regions cut to the basemap’s Natural Earth 1:50m coastlines (public domain), so they follow today’s coasts; each piece shows only in its own chapters. Parthia grows from the land round Nisa to Iran and Mesopotamia; the Seleucids shrink to Syria; Rome and Armenia appear from the meeting on the Euphrates.
