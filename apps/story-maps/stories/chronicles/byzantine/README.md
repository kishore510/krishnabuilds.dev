# The Byzantine Empire

Status: built. The eastern Roman Empire from Constantine’s new capital in 330 to the Ottoman conquest in 1453: the Hagia Sophia, the plague, Heraclius and Persia, the Arab conquests, Greek fire, the icons, Basil II, Manzikert, the Fourth Crusade, Nicaea, Mistra and the fall of the city. 13 chapters, a **Powers** layer that changes chapter by chapter, and 8 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `near-east` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter. The story sets its own whole-map view (`overview`), from Greece to northern Mesopotamia. Italy and North Africa, which Justinian won back, lie beyond the map’s western edge, and Venice is a hidden waypoint there.
- Source: chapters rest on **History**: Byzantine historians and chroniclers (Procopius for Justinian and the plague, Theophanes for the seventh and eighth centuries, John Skylitzes for Basil II, Michael Attaleiates and Psellos for Manzikert, Niketas Choniates and Geoffrey of Villehardouin for 1204, George Sphrantzes and others for 1453), Arab chroniclers for Yarmouk, and the modern record, with ancient DNA for the cause of the plague. Disputed: the death toll of the plague; the 15,000 prisoners Skylitzes says Basil II blinded; the size of the Ottoman army in 1453 (Western accounts give far larger numbers than modern estimates); and how far Leo III started Iconoclasm, since the surviving sources come from its opponents.
- Native names are in Latin letters (Medieval Greek `gkm` by default; Arabic `ar`, Middle Persian `pal`, Bulgarian `bg`, Persian `fa`, Latin `la`, Ottoman Turkish `ota`).
- Powers areas are loose regions cut to the basemap’s Natural Earth 1:50m coastlines (public domain), so they follow today’s coasts; each piece shows only in its own chapters. The Eastern Roman Empire gains and loses lands chapter by chapter, from Syria and Egypt to the strip round Constantinople and the Morea.
