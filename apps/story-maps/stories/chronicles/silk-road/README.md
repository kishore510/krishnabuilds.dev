# The Silk Road

Status: built. The overland routes between China and the Mediterranean, from the 2nd century BCE to the turn to the sea in the 15th century: Zhang Qian's mission, the Jade Gate at Dunhuang, the heavenly horses of Ferghana, silk in Rome, Palmyra's caravans, Sogdian merchants, Kumarajiva of Kucha, the smuggled silkworms, Xuanzang's journey, Tang Chang'an, the battle of Talas, paper in Baghdad, the sealed library at Dunhuang, William of Rubruck at Karakorum, Marco Polo, plague at Caffa, Clavijo at Samarkand and the roads turning to the sea. 19 chapters, a **Powers** layer (Han China, Rome, Parthia, the Kushans, Sasanian Persia, Byzantium, Tang China, the Abbasids, the Mongols and Timur's empire) and 4 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `world` basemap and the shared art library, including the `camel` traveller icon added for this story. Routes show only for the current chapter. The Black Sea to Messina voyage runs through hidden waypoints (Bosporus, Dardanelles, Aegean and Ionian seas) so it stays at sea.
- Source: every card is **History**.
- Native names are in Chinese (`zh-Hant`), with Latin, Aramaic in Latin letters, Greek, Hindi, Arabic and Persian where the place is named in them.
- Sources and what is disputed: Built from standard histories of the Silk Road and the sources the cards name: Sima Qian’s Records of the Grand Historian and the Han histories for Zhang Qian, the heavenly horses and Gan Ying; Pliny the Elder and Seneca; the Sogdian Ancient Letters; Procopius; Xuanzang’s Record of the Western Regions; William of Rubruck; Marco Polo; and Clavijo. Disputed or uncertain: the year Zhang Qian left (139 or 138 BCE) and when Dunhuang became a commandery; which sea Gan Ying reached; where Procopius’s Serinda was; the story that prisoners taken at Talas brought papermaking to Samarkand; whether corpses were really catapulted into Caffa, and the death toll of the Black Death; how much of Marco Polo’s account is first-hand; and the dates of Kumarajiva and of Xuanzang’s departure. The name ‘Silk Road’ is modern, coined in 1877. Powers areas are loose regions, not borders.
