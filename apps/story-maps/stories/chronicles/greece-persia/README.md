# Greece and Persia

Status: built. The Greek city-states and the Persian empire, and where they met: the games at Olympia (776 BCE by tradition), Delphi and the colonies, the thinkers of Miletus, Cyrus and the Medes, Croesus at Sardis, Babylon and Egypt, Darius at Behistun, Persepolis, the Royal Road, Scylax down the Indus and the bridge over the Bosporus, Athens' democracy, the Ionian Revolt, Marathon, the silver of Laurion, Xerxes at the Hellespont, Thermopylae, Salamis, Plataea and Mycale, the Delian League and the Parthenon, Persian gold and Sparta's victory, the Ten Thousand, Mausolus at Halicarnassus, Philip of Macedon, Alexander at the Granicus, Issus and Gaugamela, the burning of Persepolis, the death of Darius III, the weddings at Susa and Alexander's death in Babylon, to the first marathon in 1896 and the digging of Persepolis. 34 chapters, a **Powers** layer that changes chapter by chapter, and 19 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `near-east` basemap (widened west to take in mainland Greece) and the shared art library, with two additions: a Greek temple glyph and a trireme traveller. Routes show only for the current chapter. The story sets its own whole-map view (`overview`), from Greece to Iran.
- Source: chapters rest mostly on **History** (Greek historians, Persian and Babylonian inscriptions and tablets, and the modern record), with **Archaeology** for the Persepolis dig and **Tradition** for the date of the first Olympic games. The Greek writers were not neutral, and their figures for Persian armies are given as theirs, with modern estimates where they differ.
- Native names are in Latin letters (Greek `grc` by default; Old Persian `peo`, Elamite `elx`, Lydian `xld`, Akkadian `akk`, Egyptian `egy`).
- Five pins are moved a little from their true spots so they can be told apart: Delphi and Thermopylae, Marathon and Salamis (around Athens), and Pasargadae (beside Persepolis). The story's note says so.
- Powers areas are loose regions cut to the basemap's Natural Earth 1:50m coastlines (public domain), so they follow today's coasts; each piece shows only in its own chapters. Sparta is painted as the whole Peloponnese, and Alexander's empire leaves out the Greek cities of his league.
