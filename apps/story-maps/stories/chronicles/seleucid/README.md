# The Seleucid Empire

Status: built. The successor state of Alexander’s empire from his death at Babylon in 323 BCE to Pompey’s end of the dynasty in 64 or 63 BCE: Seleucus’s return, Seleucia and Antioch, the elephants of Ipsus, the east breaking away, Raphia, Antiochus the Great’s march to Bactria, Panium, Magnesia, the Maccabees, the Parthian conquest of Mesopotamia and Dura-Europos dug up. 15 chapters, a **Powers** layer that changes chapter by chapter, and 6 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `near-east` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes show only for the current chapter. The story sets its own whole-map view (`overview`), from the Aegean to the map’s east edge. Bactra and the Indus lie beyond that edge, so they are hidden waypoints at the edge and routes run off the map towards them.
- Source: chapters rest on **History**: Polybius for Raphia, Antiochus III’s eastern march and Magnesia; Diodorus, Appian and Justin for the Successors, Ipsus and the treaty with Chandragupta; Josephus and 1 and 2 Maccabees for Judea; Babylonian astronomical diaries and dated tablets; Pliny for Seleucia. The last chapter rests on **Archaeology** (the French and Yale excavations at Dura-Europos). Disputed: how much land Seleucus ceded to Chandragupta and what the marriage alliance was; the dates of the Bactrian and Parthian breakaways; ancient army sizes at Raphia and Magnesia; Pliny’s 600,000 for Seleucia; whether Syria became a province in 64 or 63 BCE.
- Native names are in Latin letters (Ancient Greek `grc` by default; Hebrew `he`).
- Powers areas are loose regions cut to the basemap’s Natural Earth 1:50m coastlines (public domain), so they follow today’s coasts; each piece shows only in its own chapters. The Seleucid Empire gains and loses lands chapter by chapter, from Babylonia to Iran, Syria and Anatolia, and back to the strip round Antioch.
