# The Deccan Sultanates

Status: built. The Deccan from 1347 to 1687: the revolt at Daulatabad, Gulbarga and Bidar, Mahmud Gawan's college, the break-up into five kingdoms, Golconda's diamonds, Talikota, Ibrahim II, Hyderabad and the Charminar, Chand Bibi and Malik Ambar, the Gol Gumbaz, and the Mughal conquests of Bijapur and Golconda. 16 chapters, 4 people cards and a **Powers** layer that changes lands chapter by chapter.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `south-asia` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes of earlier chapters stay on the map (`journey`).
- Source: History throughout.
- Native names are in Persian (`fa`), except chapter 8 in Kannada (`kn`).
- Sources: the Persian histories of Firishta (Tarikh-i Firishta), Ali Tabataba (Burhan-i Maasir) and Rafi al-Din Shirazi (Tazkirat al-Muluk), the Kitab-i Nauras, Portuguese accounts of Talikota by Diogo do Couto, and Mughal chronicles; standard histories by H. K. Sherwani, The Bahmanis of the Deccan, H. K. Sherwani and P. M. Joshi (eds), History of Medieval Deccan, and Richard M. Eaton, A Social History of the Deccan, 1300–1761. Disputed: Bahman Shah's origins (the tale of his service to a Brahmin named Gangu is doubted); the founding years of the five kingdoms, especially Golconda (1512 or 1518); the date of Talikota (23 or 26 January 1565) and who killed Rama Raya; whether the Koh-i-Noor came from the Kollur mines; the Bhagmati story; how Chand Bibi died; and the story of the gate opened at Golconda.
