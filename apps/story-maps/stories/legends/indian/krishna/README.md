# The Life of Krishna

Status: built. Krishna’s life as tradition tells it, in 37 chapters: the voice at Devaki’s wedding, the birth in Kamsa’s prison and the crossing of the Yamuna, Gokula (Putana, the butter thief, the mortar), Vrindavan (Kaliya, Govardhan, the rasa dance, Radha in later tradition), Akrura’s chariot and the end of Kamsa, Sandipani at Avanti, Uddhava and the song of the bee, Jarasandha’s sieges and Kalayavana, the founding of Dwaraka, Rukmini, the Syamantaka jewel, Naraka at Pragjyotisha, Usha and Aniruddha at Shonitapura, the schoolfriend’s flattened rice, Arjuna and Subhadra, the Khandava forest, Jarasandha’s end, Shishupala, the peace mission, the Bhagavad Gita, the charioteer’s war, Gandhari’s curse, the iron club, the Uddhava Gita, the hunter’s arrow at Prabhasa, the sea taking Dwaraka, and three chapters from the historical record (the Heliodorus pillar, the Gita Govinda, the 16th-century rediscovery of Vrindavan). A **Kingdoms** layer (Shurasena, Magadha, the Yadavas of Dwaraka, Kuru, and Vidarbha, Pragjyotisha, Bana’s kingdom and Chedi) and 14 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `south-asia` basemap and the shared art library, plus new art: a flute glyph for Vrindavan, and Garuda and a basket-bearer (Vasudeva carrying the baby) as travellers. Routes show only for the current chapter.
- Source: **Tradition**, except the last three chapters, which are **History**. The text follows the Bhagavata Purana (books 10 and 11), with the Mahabharata for the epic chapters, and says where they differ (where Vajra is crowned, why the Yadavas left Mathura) and where well-known figures (Radha, Sudama by name) come from later tradition.
- Places are where tradition puts them. Where several places claim one (Jambavan’s cave, Muchukunda’s cave, Shonitapura, Nanda’s home), the card names them.
- Mathura, Gokul, Vrindavan and Govardhan are within about 30 km of one another, far closer than a pin; on this map they are spread about a degree apart so each can be tapped, and the story’s note says so. Indraprastha, Hastinapura, Kurukshetra, Guwahati and Tezpur are nudged a little to make room.
- Native names are in Sanskrit (`sa`), in Devanagari, except two Hindi terms (`hi`).
