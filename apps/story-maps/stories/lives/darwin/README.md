# Charles Darwin

Status: built. In Lives. The life of Charles Darwin, 1809 to 1882, in 24 chapters: a doctor's son in Shrewsbury, medicine in Edinburgh, beetles at Cambridge, the voyage of HMS Beagle (Plymouth, St Jago, Bahia, Punta Alta, Tierra del Fuego, the Concepción earthquake, the Andes, the Galápagos, the Pacific and Sydney, Cocos coral atolls, the Cape and home to Falmouth), then the tree of life in London, Malthus and marriage, Down House, Annie at Malvern, Wallace's letter from Ternate, the Origin of Species, the Oxford debate, the years of orchids and worms, and Westminster Abbey. 8 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `world` basemap. Routes keep earlier chapters' lines (`journey`), so the Beagle's voyage builds up around the world. Places in England are closer together than a pin on this world map, so each appears only in its own chapters and no route is drawn between English places. The sea legs are bent and run through hidden waypoints (Cape Horn side, Tahiti, New Zealand, Bass Strait, Mauritius, St Helena, Ascension, the Red Sea and others) so they stay at sea; the Pacific crossing is drawn in two pieces at the map's edges.
- No new art. The Beagle uses the `merchant-ship` icon; Wallace's letter uses `scroll`.
- Source: every card is **History**.
- Native names are in Spanish (`es`), used only on South American chapters, with Portuguese (`pt`) set on the Cape Verde and Bahia chapters.
- Sources and what is disputed: Darwin's Journal of Researches (The Voyage of the Beagle), his Beagle diary, notebooks and correspondence (Darwin Correspondence Project), his Autobiography, and biographies by Janet Browne and by Adrian Desmond and James Moore. Disputed or handled with care: how much the Galápagos finches shaped his thinking at the time (he did not label most by island, and their importance emerged in London); where Wallace wrote his 1858 essay (dated Ternate, possibly written on Halmahera) and the exact date the letter reached Down; the words of the 1860 Oxford exchange, which survive only in later and differing accounts; Annie's cause of death (probably tuberculosis); the cause of Darwin's lifelong illness, which is still debated; and Darwin's harsh descriptions of the Fuegians, quoted in brief and not endorsed.
