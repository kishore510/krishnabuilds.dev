# The Napoleonic Wars

Status: built. The wars of 1793 to 1815: the siege of Toulon, the army of Italy, Egypt and the Nile, Acre, the coup of Brumaire, Marengo, the coronation, Trafalgar, Austerlitz, Jena, Tilsit, the rising in Madrid, Wagram, the Lines of Torres Vedras, the crossing of the Niemen, Borodino, Moscow and the Berezina, Leipzig, the abdication and Elba, the Hundred Days, Waterloo, the Congress of Vienna, St Helena and the return of the ashes. 25 chapters, a **Powers** layer (France, the French client states, Britain, Spain, Austria, Prussia, Russia and the Ottoman Empire) and 8 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `mediterranean` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Only the current chapter's routes are shown. St Helena, Ushant, Cape Finisterre and Le Havre are hidden waypoints; St Helena lies far off the map in the South Atlantic, so the routes to and from it run off the edge.
- Source: every card is **History**.
- Native names are in French (`fr`), with each step or person setting its own language (`it`, `ar`, `es`, `cs`, `de`, `ru`, `pt`).
- Sources: standard histories of the wars (Charles Esdaile, Napoleon’s Wars; Andrew Roberts, Napoleon the Great; David Chandler, The Campaigns of Napoleon; Adam Zamoyski, 1812 and Rites of Peace; Dominic Lieven, Russia Against Napoleon; Alexander Mikaberidze, The Napoleonic Wars: A Global History). Disputed: every army size and casualty figure, above all the Grande Armée of 1812 (450,000 to over 600,000) and the losses at Borodino and Leipzig; the number of prisoners killed at Jaffa (usually given as 2,000 to 4,000); who started the Moscow fire; the total dead of the wars (3.5 to 7 million, soldiers and civilians); how far the wars were Napoleon’s doing rather than the coalitions’. "Napoleonic Wars" strictly means 1803 to 1815; the story starts with the French Revolutionary Wars in 1793. Pins are on today’s map; Kovno is Kaunas and Tilsit is Sovetsk. The Battle of the Pyramids pin is at Embaba, where it was fought.
- Powers areas are loose regions, not borders. Some are simplified: Spain’s status in each chapter is given in its ties, and Tyrol is drawn Austrian again from the abdication chapter (1814).
