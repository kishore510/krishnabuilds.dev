# The First World War

Status: built. The war of 1914 to 1918: the shots in Sarajevo, the march through Belgium and the Marne, Tannenberg, the Indian Corps at Ypres, Gallipoli, Verdun and the Somme, the siege of Kut, America’s entry, Caporetto, Revolution and Brest-Litovsk, Amiens and the Hundred Days, Palestine and Haifa, the armistice at Compiègne and the Treaty of Versailles. 16 chapters, a **Powers** layer (Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, the occupied west and east, France and Britain, Russia and Italy) and 7 people cards.

- `story.json`: the story data. It uses the `mediterranean` basemap and the shared art library, with no additions. Routes stay on the map from chapter to chapter. Petrograd, Basra and the Atlantic crossing lie beyond the map’s edge, so those routes start from hidden waypoints.
- Source: every card is **History**.
- Native names are in French (`fr`), with each step or person setting its own language (`sr`, `de`, `tr`, `ar`, `it`, `ru`, `ur`).
- Sources: standard histories of the war (Hew Strachan, The First World War; Margaret MacMillan, The War That Ended Peace and Peacemakers; David Omissi and George Morton-Jack on the Indian Army; Commonwealth War Graves Commission records). Disputed: how far each power was to blame for the outbreak; all casualty figures, above all for Gallipoli, Verdun and the war as a whole (soldiers 9 to 10 million, civilians several million more, before counting the 1918 influenza); the number of Belgian civilians killed in 1914 (about 6,500 is the usual estimate); the prisoner count at Caporetto (260,000 to 300,000); whether Khudadad Khan or Darwan Singh Negi was the first Indian awarded the Victoria Cross (both were gazetted on 7 December 1914). The battle at Tannenberg was fought nearer Allenstein (Olsztyn); the Germans named it after Tannenberg on purpose, for the defeat of the Teutonic Knights there in 1410.
- Powers areas are loose regions, not borders, and the front lines are simplified.
