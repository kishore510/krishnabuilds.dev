# Pluto and the Kuiper Belt

Status: built. Pluto and the Kuiper belt in 11 chapters: Tombaugh’s discovery in 1930, Charon in 1978, the first Kuiper belt object in 1992, Eris and the vote of 2006, New Horizons’ launch and Jupiter swing-by, the heart of Pluto, its haze and glaciers, Charon’s red cap, Arrokoth and where New Horizons is today. 4 people cards (Tombaugh, Burney, Luu, Stern).

- `story.json`: the story data. It is on the `cosmic` canvas and uses the `solar-system` sky basemap. The map is a diagram of distance, not a picture of space: left to right is distance from the Sun on a logarithmic scale, and up and down mean nothing (Pluto and Eris are drawn off the planets’ plane only as a nod to their tilted orbits). The New Horizons legs build one path, Earth to Jupiter to Pluto to Arrokoth to today.
- Art: no new drawings; it uses the `dwarf-planet` glyph added with Asteroids and Comets.
- Source: History by default; the cards on the Kuiper belt, Pluto’s heart, haze and glaciers, Charon and Arrokoth are **Science**.
- Sources and what is uncertain: dates, distances and sizes follow NASA, the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory and Southwest Research Institute New Horizons pages, Lowell Observatory for the discovery of Pluto and the International Astronomical Union for the 2006 resolution and the names. Positions on the map are each body's distance from the Sun at the time, on the solar-system map's logarithmic scale. What is uncertain: whether Pluto has a liquid ocean under its crust, and the cause of Charon's red pole, are theories from New Horizons' data, and the cards say so; New Horizons' distance today (about 65 AU) is approximate and grows by about 3 AU a year; how long its power lasts is NASA's estimate. Whether Pluto should be called a planet is still argued; the card gives the IAU's vote and says it is disputed.
