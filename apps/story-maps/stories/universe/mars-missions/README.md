# Missions to Mars

Status: built. The missions to Mars in 21 chapters: Mariner 4's first close-ups, Mariner 9 and Valles Marineris, Mars 3's twenty seconds, Viking 1 and 2 and the search for life, Pathfinder and Sojourner, Olympus Mons, Beagle 2, ice at the south pole, Spirit, Opportunity and Endeavour crater, Phoenix, Curiosity, Mangalyaan, InSight, Perseverance at Jezero, Ingenuity, China's Zhurong and the missions still to come. 4 people cards (Levin, Squyres, Annadurai, Steltzner).

- `story.json`: the story data. It is on the `cosmic` canvas and uses the `mars` sky basemap, whose units are planetocentric longitude (east positive) and latitude, so every place is a real [lon, lat] on Mars, except the `orbit` pin, a symbolic place above the north edge for orbiters. There are no routes: every chapter has `legs: []`.
- Art added for this story: the `orbiter` and `helicopter` glyphs.
- Source: History by default, the way the missions were run and what they found; chapters 5, 7, 9, 11, 12, 16 and 20 (Viking's life experiments, Olympus Mons, the south polar ice, Opportunity, Phoenix, InSight and Jezero) are **Science**.
- Sources and what is uncertain: Landing sites, dates and distances follow NASA (JPL and the National Space Science Data Center), the Soviet space programme as reported by NSSDC, ESA, ISRO, CNSA and the UAE Space Agency, and the IAU Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature for official names (Thomas Mutch, Gerald Soffen, Carl Sagan, Columbia, Challenger Memorial Stations, Bradbury Landing, Octavia E. Butler Landing, Wright Brothers Field). Mars 3's 20 seconds are often given as 14.5; the 2013 identification of its hardware is unconfirmed. The MARSIS lake under the south polar ice (2018) is disputed. Cheyava Falls is a *potential* biosignature (NASA, 2025); non-biological chemistry could also explain it. Viking's labelled release result is still argued over by a few scientists. Chapter 21 gives plans as of November 2026 (Rosalind Franklin 2028, ESCAPADE arriving 2027, Tianwen-3 returning samples about 2031); they will change, and NASA's Mars Sample Return has been repeatedly rethought. The map is the new `mars` basemap. New art: the `orbiter` and `helicopter` glyphs.
