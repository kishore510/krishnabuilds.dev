The **Car decision** page costs up to four ways of having a car over the same stretch of time: **keep** your current car, **buy with cash**, a personal **loan**, **hire purchase** (HP) or a **PCP**. It is an illustration under your own assumptions, not advice, and it never says which option to choose: it shows which has the **lowest net cost under these assumptions**, and what would change that. It **uses no tax rules**, so it has no tax year and shows "Uses no tax rules" where other pages show when their tax figures were last verified.

## Your options

Each option is a row with its own colour and an icon for how you get the car, its name, a short summary and, once it has figures, its average cost a month. Tap a row to open it and fill it in; one opens at a time, and what you typed stays when you close it. A row that still needs boxes says how many.

- **Start from** sets up a common comparison in one go: **Keep or replace** (your car against a new one on PCP), **Cash or finance** (one car three ways) or **Compare finance offers** (a loan, HP and PCP). It sets how each car is had and its name only, with every amount empty, and replaces your options: **Undo** puts them back.
- **Copy the car from** (inside an option) takes another option's car into this one: its price, end values, mileage, fuel and running costs, but not its name or finance. Fill in one car, then copy it into the others to compare finance on the same car without typing it again. **Undo** puts this option's own car back.
- **Add an option** adds an empty one; **Duplicate** copies one to change a single thing; **Remove** takes one away, with **Undo**.

## For every option

- **Over how many months?**: every option is costed over the same horizon. Type it in months or years, or use the slider; years are whole years.
- **Annual mileage**: the miles a year every option drives. An option that drives a different distance can have its own (below).
- **Shared assumptions**: the **savings return (after tax, a year)**, what your money would earn if it weren't spent on the car, after any tax on it: your own assumption, the same for every option. It gives the **interest your cash could have earned** on the cash paid out. And the **yearly increase in running costs**: how much insurance, maintenance, VED and other costs rise each year, for every option. Results wait until both are entered.

## Inside an option

Give it a name (optional) and choose how you get the car. Then fill in its sections; each header says what is entered, or what is still needed. The **optional costs and fees** (insurance, maintenance, VED, other costs, a deposit, an arrangement fee, an HP balloon, option and purchase fees, a PCP's excess mileage charge) show a 0 in an empty box: **an empty box counts as nothing**. Each card lists the running costs you left empty, and a line under the answer says so when the options differ in which are empty. The price, the expected end value, the mileage, the fuel figures, and for finance the term and the rate or payment (and a PCP's final payment and mileage allowance) are needed: until they are filled, the option says which.

- **The car**: its price (for keep, **What is the car worth today?**: what it would sell for today) and its **expected value at the end** of the horizon. Between today and the end, the value falls (or rises) by the same percentage every year: an illustrative shape, not a forecast. Keeping a car counts its value as a sale given up, so keeping a car and buying the same car for the same money cost the same.
- **Add a low and high end value** (optional): what the car could be worth at the end if things go badly or well. What a car is worth at the end is usually the most uncertain figure here, so once any option has a low or high value the page shows the **low, expected and high cases** and lets you choose which the results use. A low or high value left empty reads as the expected one; turning the switch off clears them.
- **Drives a different distance**: off, the option drives the shared annual mileage; on, it has its own.
- **Finance on a car you keep**: if you still owe a loan, HP or PCP on your current car, turn on **Still owe finance on it?** and fill in four boxes: what the lender says you owe to clear it today (the **settlement figure**: use the lender's current settlement quote), how much you pay each month, how many payments are left, and any final payment due at the end (an empty box counts as nothing). The first payment is taken as a month from today; the final payment is paid with the last one and you keep the car. Keeping then counts what a sale today would leave after clearing the finance (the car's value less the settlement) as a sale given up; if you owe more than the car is worth, the card flags **negative equity now** and that figure is below nothing. Only the interest in the payments is a cost: the rest pays down what you owe. If the horizon ends before the payments do, what is still owed then is taken off the car's value. If the payments and final payment add up to less than the settlement, there are no results for that option and it says why.
- **Finance** (loan, HP and PCP): the loan amount or the deposit, the term in months, and either the interest rate with its basis (effective a year, or nominal with a twelfth charged each month) or the monthly payment from a quote. An arrangement fee is paid at the start or added to what you borrow. HP can have a balloon and an option to purchase fee. A PCP has an optional final payment and a purchase fee, and you choose to **buy the car** or **hand it back** at the end; handing it back pays any excess mileage over the allowance.
- **Running costs**: insurance, maintenance, **VED** and other costs a year, for the first year. VED is an amount you enter: check the rate for the car on GOV.UK, as Sumyard holds no VED figures. Fuel or energy is **petrol or diesel** (miles per gallon and the price per litre), **electric** (miles per kWh, the share charged at home, and the home and public prices) or your own **cost per mile**; the page shows the cost per mile it works out. Fuel or energy has its own yearly price change.

## The answer

The answer sits beside your options on a laptop, where it stays in view as you scroll, and after them on a phone; on a phone the bar at the top carries it in short.

- **The headline** is how much less a month the option with the lowest net cost is than the next option, with the total over the horizon and the savings return it uses under it (when that is nothing, tying up cash costs nothing in the comparison). Ties are stated. There is **no verdict** when there is only one option, when fewer than two options cover the whole horizon, or when an option that covers less (a PCP handed back early) has a lower average cost per month owned: such an option is never compared by total. The reason is said in full.
- **A bar for each option**, in its colour, of its average cost per month owned, lowest first, under these assumptions. An option that covers less than the horizon comes after the others and says it isn't compared by total; one without results yet says what it needs. **Show as a table** gives every option's figures side by side.
- **Switching**: when one option is **Keep current car**, a line for each other option that covers the horizon says what switching to it costs a month more or less than keeping, and over the horizon.

Below, sections with a one-line summary each hold the detail; tap one to open it.

## Where the money goes

**Net cost** is the cash you pay out, less what the car is worth at the end after anything still owed on it, plus the interest your cash could have earned. A bar for each option splits its net cost into parts, each in its own colour and icon: **depreciation** (what the car loses in value), **interest and fees**, **equity handed back**, **fuel or energy**, **running costs** (insurance, maintenance, VED and other costs together) and the **interest your cash could have earned**. The bars share one scale, so they compare. A part below nothing (a car that gains value, say) is said under its bar. **Show as a table** lists every part and the total for each option.

Then a card for each option:

- its **average cost per month owned** (the net cost divided by the months the option covers), the **total net cost** and its three largest parts;
- **Yours at the end**: what the car is worth then, less anything still owed on it. If more is owed than it is worth, the card flags **negative equity**; a car handed back has nothing at the end;
- **Covers** (its coverage): said only when a PCP is handed back before the horizon ends. Then the card flags it: no replacement car is modelled, so the months after are not covered, and the option is **never ordered against options that cover the whole horizon**;
- the rate (below), and the running costs you left empty;
- **Money in and out**: the cash that changes hands, apart from the net cost: **cash paid out** over the months covered, the **upfront cash** (the price for cash; the deposit and any fee paid at the start for finance; nothing for keep, whose value counts in the net cost as a sale given up), the **monthly payment** (finance only, including finance still owed on a car you keep, with how many payments are left) and, for a PCP you buy at the end, HP with a balloon, or a kept car's final payment, the **final payment due at the end** with any fee paid with it.

## Month by month

A chart shows each option, in its colour and its own line pattern, month by month from the start to the horizon. Choose **Net cost so far** (the cash paid out so far, plus any sale given up, less what the car is worth then after anything owed, plus the interest your cash could have earned so far) or **Cash paid so far** (deposits, payments, fuel and running costs, leaving out what the car is still worth, so it is not the cost). A PCP handed back before the horizon stops at the month it goes back; the horizon and each finance term are marked and listed under the chart. Where two net cost lines cross, their running totals swap over; the answer is still the one at the horizon.

**Pick a month** by tapping the chart, with the arrow keys (Home and End for the first and last month) once the chart has focus, or with a month's button in **Show the data as a table**. A small table shows, for each option at that month, the net cost and cash paid so far and what is yours then, with everything else as entered. It starts at the horizon; the month you pick isn't saved with a scenario.

## What changes the answer

Choose two options that cover the whole horizon with **First option** and **Second option**; it starts with the two lowest. Both parts of this section use the same two.

- **Where the difference comes from**: the first option's average cost per month owned less the second's, split into the parts the engine gives (interest and fees, depreciation, equity handed back, fuel or energy, insurance, maintenance, VED, other and the interest your cash could have earned). Each part is the difference between the two options' parts, divided by the months owned, to the penny, so the parts add up to the difference, which is the answer's gap a month. A sentence names the largest part ("Most of the difference is …" when it is more than half of the difference and on the same side), and bars go right of a centre line for parts that are more for the first option and left for less; **Show as a table** lists every part. It describes the difference; it doesn't say why.
- **Thresholds**: once your inputs settle, the point at which the two would swap: each one's end value, your savings return, and the annual mileage **if both cars are driven the same distance, as one driver**. Each line changes one thing and holds everything else as entered. Where nothing in the range searched would swap them, it says **no crossing in range**. The thresholds are always measured over the whole horizon, whatever month the chart shows. A last line gives the **fuel or energy price** at which the order would swap: the first option's cost per mile, in pence per mile, with its mileage, its yearly fuel price change and everything else as entered; for petrol or diesel also as a price per litre, from the car's miles per gallon; for an electric car pence per mile only, home and public charging together, with the split as you entered it. It is worked out a moment after the other lines.

Near a threshold the two totals, each rounded to the penny, can be level or pennies apart either way over a narrow band. The search carries on past that band and names the option lower on each side; each value is "about", and where the band shows as a range the line gives it.

## How sure is this

- **Low, expected and high end values**: once an option has a low or high end value, each option's average cost per month owned in all three cases, one row each, a sentence saying whether the option with the lowest net cost changes between them, and the choice of which case the page shows. Until then this says how to add a range.
- **What moves each option's cost most**: for each option, how its average cost per month owned would change if one input moved by a fixed percentage up or down, **one at a time, with every other input held at its current value**: the end value (in the case chosen), the savings return, the annual mileage and the fuel or energy price. The inputs that move it most come first, with a small bar for the largest moves either way. A move that would take an input past what Sumyard models says **Outside the range**. It is worked out when you open it, once you stop typing ("Updating" until then), since it reruns every option many times. The changes are constant percentages of today's inputs: illustrative only, not a forecast and not advice.

## Rates

For a loan, and for HP with no option to purchase fee, the card shows the **APR** worked out from the agreement's payments under the APR rules. For a PCP, and HP with an option fee, whether the APR counts the optional final payment or the fee isn't verified yet, so the card shows the **effective rate with every fee, not the lender’s APR**: the rate at which every payment, fees included, pays off what you borrowed. A PCP at no interest with a purchase fee therefore shows a small rate above nothing: that is the fee. A line under it says which fees it includes: any arrangement or documentation fee, and a PCP's final payment and purchase fee, or HP's balloon and option to purchase fee.

For finance still owed on a car you keep, the card shows the **implied rate from your figures, not the lender’s APR**: the yearly rate at which your payments and any final payment repay the settlement figure. It can differ from your agreement's APR, for example when the settlement figure differs from the balance your agreement's own schedule would show.

## Share

**Share** beside the page title makes a copy of the answer to send: an image card, a PDF (print to PDF), a spreadsheet (CSV) or text to paste. It shows the option with the lowest average cost per month owned and the gap to the next, each option's cost a month and in all, where the lowest option's money goes beside the other options' figures, and the switch cost against keeping; not your prices, finance terms, running costs, the thresholds or the chart. Turn **Show amounts** off to share each option as a percentage above the lowest, and the parts as shares, with no pound figure. Turn **Show option names** off to name each option by how the car is had (such as PCP or Loan) instead. It is made on your device; nothing is uploaded and nothing is stored.

## When there are no results

If a box isn't valid yet, it says why and only that option waits (a shared box holds back every option). If the figures don't work for an option (a deposit as large as the price, a loan above the price, an end value too high for the price), it says which field and why, and shows no number. The other options keep their results.

## Not modelled

Tax of any kind, VED rules, electric car tax or benefit rules, company cars, a replacement car after a hand back, ending an agreement early or settling it (for finance on a car you keep, the settlement figure is the lender's: early settlement charges and rights are not worked out), handing back a PCP you already have, a new agreement that pays off the old one, part exchange, leasing, condition charges when a car is handed back, and inflation (amounts are in pounds of the day). Scenarios save the inputs, including the case shown and the shared mileage, and compare works as on other pages (see [Scenarios and saving](help:scenarios-and-saving)). Scenarios saved before this version open with the same figures: the first mileage entered becomes the shared one, and any option that drove a different distance keeps its own. The assumptions on the page list everything.
